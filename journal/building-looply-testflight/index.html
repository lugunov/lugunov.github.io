Building Looply: From a Small Annoyance to Something I Actually Use

It started with salt. Now it is a small personal logbook for the repetitive parts of life.

Looply started with salt.

Not exactly the most exciting origin story for a mobile app, I know.

We have a water softener at home, and every so often I need to refill it with salt. The problem is that “every so often” isn't a particularly useful reminder interval.

Sometimes the salt lasts 20 days. Sometimes 18. Sometimes less.

I tried normal recurring reminders, but they were always slightly wrong. Either the reminder appeared when there was still plenty of salt left, or I remembered about it after it had already run out.

And at some point I had a simple thought:

Why should I tell the app when something will happen again?

Why not just tell it when something did happen?

First refill.

Second refill.

Now there is an interval.

After the third refill, there is more history.

And gradually the app can estimate when the next one is likely to happen.

That was the original idea behind Looply.

Then I started finding more things to put into it

Once I had a working version on my phone, something interesting happened.

I started noticing possible Looply use cases everywhere.

Water filters.

Household supplies.

Maintenance.

Things I replace.

Things I clean.

Things I check.

Things I buy again and again.

Yes, even toilet paper.

The basic pattern is incredibly common:

Something happens repeatedly, but not precisely enough to justify a calendar event.

And this is where Looply started becoming useful to me personally.

I don't really need another calendar.

I don't need another giant task management system.

I just want to quickly record:

“I did this today.”

And let the history take care of the rest.

The Events screen unexpectedly became one of my favourite parts

Originally, I was mostly interested in prediction.

Now I find myself opening Events almost as often.

Maybe that's because I'm slightly obsessive about data and statistics. 🙂

Events gives me a simple historical timeline of what I actually did.

When did I replace that filter?

When did I refill the salt?

When was the last time I did some maintenance?

Instead of trying to remember, I can just look.

And after you have been recording something for a while, that little history becomes surprisingly useful.

It is almost like a tiny personal logbook for the boring repetitive parts of life.

And apparently I like logbooks more than I realised.

The nice thing is that Looply doesn't really care what you're tracking

That is probably what I like most about the idea now.

Looply doesn't need to understand whether an event is about a water softener, a household item, maintenance, a hobby or something completely different.

It only understands:

it happened.

And then it happened again.

From there, history and intervals start becoming useful.

The more I use it myself, the more I realise that the potential use cases are much broader than the original idea.

And I deliberately don't want to turn it into a huge application with dozens of specialised features.

My original principle was:

Make it simple now. There will always be time to make it complicated later.

Looply is intentionally simple.

No cloud.

No subscription.

Your data stays locally on your phone.

I will probably add data export/import and improve things based on real feedback, but I want to keep the basic idea intact:

Record what happened.

See your history.

Let Looply estimate what might happen next.

That's it.

It started because I kept running out of salt.

Now I'm mostly curious to see what else people will use it for.
