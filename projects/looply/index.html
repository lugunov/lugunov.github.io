Looply

A small product built to solve a very ordinary problem: remembering the things that repeat in life.

Visit Looply ↗

Why I built it

Some things repeat without belonging on a particular date. Replacing filters, household maintenance, recurring purchases, servicing equipment, personal routines — their timing often depends on usage and the rhythm of real life.

A calendar asks for a date. A traditional to-do app asks for a task and often a fixed repeat interval. I wanted something that could start with what actually happened, without requiring me to invent the perfect schedule first.

The idea

log → learn → predict

Record an action when it happens. Over time, those small records reveal a rhythm. Looply uses that history to help predict when the action may be needed again.

The prediction is a useful starting point, not a promise. Life changes, and the next time something happens is another opportunity to learn.

Product principles

  • Simple first. Make the everyday action clear before adding options.
  • Low-friction logging. Recording something should take less effort than trying to remember it.
  • Local-first / privacy-conscious. Keep personal routines on the device wherever possible and be deliberate about data.
  • Avoid over-engineering. Add complexity only when a real problem earns it.
  • Build based on real usage. Let everyday experience and feedback shape what comes next.

How I built it

I am building Looply with Flutter for iOS, using GitHub to manage the code and changes. TestFlight and preparation for the App Store are part of turning a working app into something other people can install and use.

AI-assisted development — sometimes called vibe coding — is part of the process. I use Codex as a development partner to explore approaches, work on implementation, and help investigate problems. I still make the product decisions, review changes, and check how the app behaves in practice.

What I learned

  • Shipping is different from designing: the small unfinished details become real work.
  • App Store distribution has its own complexity, beyond writing the app.
  • Real user feedback changes priorities — often towards the everyday basics.
  • Simplicity is hard. It takes repeated decisions about what to leave out.

Current status

Looply is being prepared for release on the App Store. The product website is the place to follow its availability and learn more about the app.
